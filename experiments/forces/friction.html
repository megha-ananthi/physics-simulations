💡 What did we discover?

  • Friction opposes relative motion — it acts even when the block is NOT moving (static friction balances your pull).
  • Static > Sliding > Rolling — it takes more force to START moving than to KEEP moving, and rolling beats sliding by far. That is why we invented the wheel and ball bearings!
  • Friction depends on how rough the surfaces are and how hard they press together.
  • Liquids and gases push back too — that is fluid friction (drag). It grows with speed, which is why falling objects reach a steady terminal velocity. Streamlined shapes cut through with less drag.
  • Friction is a necessary evil: we could not walk, write or brake without it, but it also wears out shoe soles and machine parts and wastes energy as heat.
🪀 Carrom powder = less friction 🚲 Ball bearings in cycle wheels 👟 Shoe treads = more grip 🏏 Streamlined cricket ball swing ✈️ Aeroplanes are streamlined 🔥 Rubbing palms makes heat
