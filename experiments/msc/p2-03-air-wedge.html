🔍 Air Wedge — Thickness of a Thin Wire

🏠 Lab

Two optically plane glass plates touch at one end and a thin wire props them apart at the other, trapping a wedge of air. Sodium light reflected from the top and bottom of this film interferes, giving straight, equally spaced fringes. Measure the fringe width β with a travelling microscope and the wire’s thickness follows: t = Lλ / 2β.

Inside the air film

① reflects at P, the lower surface of the upper plate (glass → air): no phase change.
② reflects at Q, the upper surface of the lower plate (air → glass): phase change of π, i.e. an extra λ/2.

Path difference Δ = 2t + λ/2 (air, normal incidence)

Dark fringe: Δ = (2n + 1)λ/2 ⇒ 2t = nλ  ·  Bright fringe: 2t = (2n − 1)λ/2

At the tied end t = 0, so Δ = λ/2 and the edge of contact is dark. Along a line parallel to that edge t is constant, so each fringe is a straight line; t grows uniformly with distance, so the fringes are equally spaced.

THICKNESS OF A THIN WIRE — AIR WEDGE

Aim

To calculate the thickness of a thin wire by forming interference fringes using an air wedge arrangement.

Apparatus required

Two optically plane glass plates, sodium vapour lamp, a thin wire, condensing lens, travelling microscope, reading lens.

Formula

Thickness of the thin wire  t = Lλ2β (m)

where L = distance between the tied end and the wire (m), β = fringe width (m), λ = wavelength of the sodium light (5893 × 10−10 m).

Least count of the travelling microscope

Least count (LC) = value of 1 main scale division (MSD) / number of divisions on the vernier

20 MSD = 1 cm  ⇒  value of 1 MSD = 1/20 cm = 0.05 cm

Number of divisions on the vernier = 50

LC = 0.05 / 50 = 0.001 cm

Principle

When a wedge-shaped air film between two optically plane glass plates is illuminated normally with monochromatic light, the rays reflected from the lower surface of the upper plate and from the upper surface of the lower plate interfere. Their path difference is 2t + λ/2 (the λ/2 comes from the phase change of π on reflection at the denser lower plate), so a dark fringe is formed wherever 2t = nλ. Since the thickness of the film is constant along lines parallel to the edge of contact and increases uniformly away from it, the fringes are straight, parallel to the edge and equally spaced (fringes of equal thickness). The fringe width is β = λ / (2 tan θ), and with tan θ = t / L the thickness of the wire is t = Lλ / 2β.

Diagram

Procedure

  1. Two optically plane glass plates are placed one over the other and tied at one end. The given wire is introduced near the other end, so that an air wedge is formed.
  2. The distance L between the wire and the tied end is measured using a scale.
  3. Light from a sodium vapour lamp, made parallel by a condensing lens, is incident on a plane glass plate inclined at 45° to the horizontal.
  4. The light reflected from the plane glass plate is incident normally on the optically plane glass plates forming the air wedge and is reflected back.
  5. The reflected light from the air wedge is viewed through the eyepiece of the travelling microscope. The microscope is moved up and down and adjusted until clear interference fringes of alternate dark and bright bands are seen.
  6. The microscope is fixed so that the vertical cross-wire coincides with a dark band (say the nth band) and the reading is noted.
  7. The microscope is moved across the fringes and readings are noted when the vertical cross-wire coincides with the (n+5)th, (n+10)th … (n+70)th dark bands.
  8. The observed readings are tabulated; the width of 30 fringes S is found and the band width β = S / 30 is calculated.
  9. The thickness of the given wire is calculated using the formula t = Lλ / 2β.

Precautions

  1. The glass plates must be optically plane and clean; the wire is placed parallel to the tied edge.
  2. The microscope is focused on the air film until sharp dark and bright bands are seen, with the vertical cross-wire parallel to the fringes.
  3. The microscope screw is turned in one direction only while taking readings, to avoid backlash error.
  4. The fringes are counted carefully without missing any, and the cross-wire is set at the centre of each dark band.
  5. L is measured from the edge of contact (tied end) to the wire.
