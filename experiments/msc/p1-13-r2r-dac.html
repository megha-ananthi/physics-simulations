OP-AMP 4-BIT DIGITAL TO ANALOG CONVERTER — R-2R LADDER

Aim

To design an R-2R ladder 4-bit digital-to-analog (D/A) converter using IC 741 and to verify its output voltage for all binary inputs from 0000 to 1111.

Apparatus required

Op-amp IC 741, resistors R = 12 kΩ and 2R = 24 kΩ, breadboard, dual regulated power supply (0 – 30 V), digital multimeter, connecting wires.

Formula

Vo = − VR (Rf / 2R) (d1/2 + d2/4 + d3/8 + d4/16)
Resolution = VFS / (2n − 1)

where VR = reference voltage (5 V), Rf = feedback resistance (= 2R), d1 = MSB and d4 = LSB (each 0 or 1), VFS = full-scale output (input 1111) and n = number of bits (4).

Circuit diagram

Theory

Real-world signals are analog, so digital systems use digital-to-analog converters to drive them. In an R-2R ladder only two resistor values, R and 2R, are used, which makes it easy to build accurately and suitable for integrated circuits. Each bit switches its 2R leg to either VR (bit = 1) or ground (bit = 0). Looking into any node of the ladder the resistance is R, so the contribution of each bit is halved from the MSB to the LSB. By the principle of superposition the output is the weighted sum of the inputs, and the op-amp (inverting amplifier with virtual ground) converts it into a voltage. The output is linearly proportional to the binary input, and its range can be changed through VR.

Procedure

  1. Wire the R-2R ladder 4-bit DAC circuit on the breadboard as shown in the circuit diagram.
  2. Select the values of R and 2R (R = 12 kΩ, 2R = 24 kΩ) and Rf = 2R.
  3. Connect ±12 V to pins 7 and 4 of IC 741 and set the reference voltage VR = 5 V.
  4. Apply the digital inputs d1 d2 d3 d4 from 0000 to 1111 by connecting each input to VR (logic 1) or ground (logic 0).
  5. Measure the output voltage Vo for each combination with the digital multimeter.
  6. Compare the observed values with the calculated values and plot the DAC characteristic.

Precautions

  1. Use close-tolerance resistors so that the ratio R : 2R is exact.
  2. Check the IC 741 pin connections and the ±VCC supply before switching on.
  3. Connect the unused inputs firmly to ground (logic 0); never leave them floating.
