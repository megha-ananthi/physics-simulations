    STUDY OF RS, CLOCKED RS, D, JK AND T FLIP-FLOPS USING NAND AND NOR GATES

    Aim

    To construct the RS (using NOR and NAND gates), clocked RS, D, JK and T flip-flops with logic gates and to verify their state (truth) tables.

    Apparatus required

    IC 7400 (quad 2-input NAND), IC 7402 (quad 2-input NOR), IC 7410 (triple 3-input NAND, for the JK and T flip-flops), digital trainer kit (+5 V supply, logic input switches, LED indicators and a debounced clock pulser), connecting leads.

    Theory

    A flip-flop is a bistable circuit that stores one bit. Its two outputs Q and Q are normally complements of each other. Two cross-coupled NOR (or NAND) gates form the basic RS latch: S = 1 sets Q = 1, R = 1 resets Q = 0, S = R = 0 keeps the previous state, and S = R = 1 is forbidden (both outputs become equal, and the state after the inputs are released together is unpredictable). In the NAND latch the inputs are active-low (S, R).

    In the clocked RS flip-flop two steering NAND gates pass S and R to the latch only while CLK = 1; when CLK = 0 the flip-flop holds its state. The D flip-flop feeds D to S and D to R, so S = R = 1 can never occur and Q follows D when clocked. In the JK flip-flop the outputs are fed back to the steering gates, so J = K = 1 makes the output toggle instead of being forbidden. If the clock stays high longer than the propagation delay, Q toggles again and again — the race-around condition. The T flip-flop is a JK flip-flop with J = K = T; it toggles on every clock pulse when T = 1.

    Characteristic equations:   RS: Qn+1 = S + RQn (SR = 0)  ·  D: Qn+1 = D  ·  JK: Qn+1 = JQn + KQn  ·  T: Qn+1 = T ⊕ Qn

    Procedure

    1. Check the ICs and place them on the digital trainer. Connect pin 14 of every IC to +5 V (VCC) and pin 7 to ground.
    2. Wire the RS flip-flop as shown in the logic diagram, using the pin numbers marked on the diagram. Connect the inputs to the logic switches and Q, Q to the LED indicators.
    3. For the clocked circuits connect CLK to the debounced pulser (monopulse key) of the trainer.
    4. Apply each input combination of the truth table (with Qn = 0 and with Qn = 1), give a clock pulse where needed and note the output Qn+1.
    5. Repeat for the clocked RS, D, JK and T flip-flops and compare the observations with the truth tables.

    Precautions

    1. Make the connections according to the IC pin diagram; note that the outputs of IC 7402 are on pins 1, 4, 10 and 13, not 3, 6, 8 and 11 as in IC 7400.
    2. The connections should be tight; VCC (pin 14) and ground (pin 7) should be applied carefully at the specified pins only.
    3. Use the debounced pulser for the clock — a mechanical switch bounces and gives several pulses.
    4. Do not leave TTL inputs open (an open input behaves as logic 1); avoid the forbidden input S = R = 1.
