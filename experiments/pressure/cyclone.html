💡 What did we discover?

  • Cyclones are large storms that form over warm ocean waters (about 27 °C or warmer). The warm, moist air above the sea rises.
  • As the moist air rises, water vapour condenses into raindrops and releases heat. The air warms, rises even further and the pressure drops even lower.
  • Air from the surrounding high-pressure regions rushes in and also rises. Earth's rotation makes the moving air spin. Without rotation there are clouds and rain but no cyclone.
  • The eye at the centre has the lowest pressure and calm wind. The wall of clouds around it has the strongest winds and the heaviest rain.
  • Strong winds push sea water onto the shore as a storm surge, a wall of water 3–12 m high. Heavy rain floods rivers. Once over land, its supply of moist air is cut off and it weakens.
  • Stay safe: follow India Meteorological Department (IMD) warnings, keep an emergency kit ready and move to the nearest cyclone shelter.
