💡 What did we discover?

  • The storm engine: when land gets heated, warm moist air rises and makes a low-pressure area. Cooler air from the surrounding high-pressure areas flows in, gets heated and rises too, so the wind keeps circulating.
  • Rising air expands and cools, and its moisture condenses into water droplets that make clouds. The droplets merge into heavier drops that fall as rain, hail or snow. Warm air can rise so high that the cold turns droplets into ice particles.
  • Strong winds blowing up and down rub ice particles and water droplets together, so charges build up. Light ⊕ ice particles go to the top of the cloud, heavy ⊖ droplets stay at the bottom, and the ground, trees and buildings below become ⊕.
  • Air is normally an insulator. When the charge build-up becomes very large, the air breaks down and a sudden flow of charges makes lightning: within a cloud, between clouds, or between a cloud and the ground. The heated air expands with a loud sound: thunder. Light reaches you almost at once, but sound covers only about 1 km every 3 seconds.
  • Stay safe: keep away from tall objects, crouch down low in a low-lying open area and do not lie flat. Avoid umbrellas with a metal rod, get out of water, and remember that a car, a bus or a building is safer.
  • A lightning conductor is a metal rod with a pointed end kept higher than the highest point of the building. Its other end is buried deep in the ground, giving the charges an easy path into the earth.
