Air is made of tiny particles that zoom about and keep hitting every surface. Those hits are air pressure. More particles in a space means more hits — higher pressure. And air always rushes from high pressure to low pressure.

💡 What did we discover?

  • Air exerts pressure on everything — the pressure of the air around us is called atmospheric pressure.
  • The air column above a 15 cm × 15 cm patch pushes with about 2250 N — like a 225 kg load. We are not crushed because the pressure inside our body is equal and balances it.
  • Air pushes in all directions, so a balloon grows equally all round.
  • Air moves from high pressure to low pressure — air rushes out of an open balloon or a punctured cycle tube.
  • A sucker sticks when the air under it is pushed out: the higher pressure outside holds it on. On a rough surface air leaks back in and it falls off.
  • Practical unit of air pressure: the millibar (mb) = 100 Pa, also written as hectopascal (hPa). Air pressure at sea level is about 1013 mb.
